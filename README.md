## Play Test 1

[Session Notes](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1oIDIbBG-6poHhfvckdD7z4yWDyml5IpCShqsbLf04UM/edit?usp=sharing)

### Questions

#### How much did you interact with your fellow players?

This is meant to be a cooperative and competitive game, so we wanted to see how they felt the structure of the game affected their social dynamics in real life with others.

#### What do you think this game is about?

We wanted the player's interpretation on what the narrative of the game was. We haven't provided any explicit cues yet, but will in future iterations of the game.

#### How difficult is it to learn the controls?

We wanted to know how intuitive the controls were and whether they affected player immersion.

#### How smooth is the gameplay?

This was a similar concern to the one outlined above.

#### How fun is the game?

No matter how thoughtful or introspective a game is, a sense of enjoyment and intrigue is paramount. 


### Observations
#### After the playthrough of the game, the players opted for free-form conversation rather than completing the Google form. The full transcript is in the appendix, but the high-level ideas are listed below.

The Good

####- The players enjoyed the feedback the game provided, including the camera spotlight flashing. They didn’t exactly know what it meant, which is potentially good for our use case and adds to the mystery of the gameplay. 
- They mentioned NOT to remove the cone. 
- The cone made the gameplay experience more engaging and and enabled them to think more strategically
#### - Player 1 liked how difficult it was to get the object into the goal slot. This was something we were going to patch, but now we might reconsider that. 
#### - Players enjoyed the stealing mechanic as it added tension and fun to the game
#### - The players both responded positively to the over visual design and 3D aspect of the game
#### - The game was able to create discussion between the players while they were competing which showed that players were engaged with the social mechanics

The Interesting

#### - The test players focused on competing with one another rather than focusing on the resource chain. In fact, it was unclear to them that there was a resource chain at all. 
- They often would drag the first step of the chain to the point bin or use steal and give commands rather than completing other steps of the resource chain to gain more points 
#### - Player 1 asked if we could utilize the z-axis of movement more, given that we were working in 3D. 
#### - Players asked if we could utilize the vast terrain instead of limiting space to the plane
#### - This might be due to lack of instruction on our part, but they didn’t seem to understand the overall larger ideas of the game (the tension between social capital v. social good). However, with a more clearly defined idea of story building off the base mechanic, we can flesh this out.
- We aim to add an instruction document to make gameplay more understandable and less experimental
#### - Players were confused about the community members and what function they served
- Player 1 mistook the community members for interactable objects/resources and suggested making them more meaningful (through multipliers or visually following the players around)

#### The Ugly
#### - Some controller-related changes led to bugs, so we reverted to an earlier version for playtesting.
#### - The gameplay was a little too fast to really introspect about each action in depth. 
- “I just think about the cameraman while trying to grab it,” says Player 2. “I just feel like on the real TV show, I have some thinking process. I see the cameraman. I think what I should do right now, because he's trying to drop it.”
- The cameraman mechanic seemed to lead to reactive behaviour rather than a more deliberate decision-making process and partially limited the opportunities for strategic gameplay 
#### - Need more visual feedback to indicate what objects are grabbable and interactable
- Player 1: “Right now, it's where you just do what you do. You're kind of running around until you see a little prompt that tells you to pick up. I was running around spamming it, just to figure out what I could do.”
#### - The scoring system was a little oblique. 
- Player 2: “I couldn’t even tell I was losing points because I had zero.”
- A solution to this would be to start from some base amount of followers.

#### Next Steps

- Add glow/shimmer for interactable objects.
- Introduce dual scoring UI (visual social capital vs. community score).
- Slow down gameplay and tune timing adjustments.
- Expand the arena to a terrain so that the players can interact in a larger world.
- Position the goals to encourage more difficult tradeoffs in terms of playing for community versus social capital scores. 
- Add a base level of social capital in the very start of the game. 
- Add an instructions sheet/tutorial round for clearer gameplay instructions.

### Reflection
#### Through our interviews, we noticed that players seemed to balance their reputation vs helping the community primarily through the first step of the resource chain (donating water) and through stealing. 

When watching the playtesting videos and interviewing players, we noticed that players did not seem especially motivated to help their community, but did feel the importance of winning, e.g. getting the highest number of points. However, this emphasis on winning almost always resulted in players stealing from one another rather than displaying cooperative or community-oriented behaviors. In the future, perhaps making the community a larger part of the narrative (through cutscenes or bonuses in a multi-round structure), might motivate players to make varied decisions in the future. 

Additionally, we did notice that once players understood how the game structure worked (e.g. Selena, who played the game around 4 times), the addition of the cameraman did drastically impact player behavior. We’re hoping that through a tutorial round and more clear signposting around the detrimental effects of being “caught” during gameplay that the cameraman’s impact will be more communicable early on. 

### Next Steps
#### We’re working to implement the feedback we received this week. Critically, we wanted to implement the spotlight rather than the cameraman, fixing the jump mechanic, and adding some sort of mechanism to limit how many steals a player can make each round. 

In terms of long-term goals, we want to iron out the playback screens and add more “juice” to them, considering this is our core mechanic. Additionally, we were thinking of adding a yodel mechanism, where we can summon the attention of the cameraman.
